# The Mirevale Aqueduct Authority — Operations Handbook (fictional)

## Founding
The Mirevale Aqueduct Authority was founded in 1871 by engineer Ilse Varga to carry water from the Korran Hills to the city of Mirevale, a distance of 42 kilometres. The original channel was built from limestone blocks quarried at Dunmere.

## Water treatment
Water entering the Authority's network passes through three stages: coarse screening at the Korran intake, slow sand filtration at the Halden works, and ultraviolet disinfection before distribution. Chlorine is added only as a residual disinfectant at 0.3 milligrams per litre.

## Capacity
The aqueduct delivers an average of 180 million litres per day. Peak summer demand reaches 240 million litres per day, which is met by drawing on the Halden reservoir, whose usable storage is 9 billion litres.

## Maintenance schedule
Each section of the channel is inspected every 18 months. Sections older than 100 years are inspected every 9 months. Emergency repairs must begin within 6 hours of a leak exceeding 50 litres per second being confirmed.

## Pricing
Households pay a fixed monthly charge of 12 crowns plus 1.4 crowns per cubic metre. Industrial users pay 2.1 crowns per cubic metre with no fixed charge. Prices are reviewed every April by the Mirevale Water Council.

## Governance
The Authority is governed by a board of seven members: four appointed by the city council, two elected by ratepayers, and one chief engineer who chairs technical committees but cannot vote on pricing.

## Drought protocol
When the Halden reservoir falls below 35 percent, stage one restrictions ban garden irrigation between 9am and 6pm. Below 20 percent, stage two restrictions cap household use at 120 litres per person per day.

## Environmental programme
Since 2009 the Authority has restored 14 kilometres of the Korran river bank and installed fish ladders at both weirs, allowing brown trout to return to the upper Korran for the first time since 1890.
